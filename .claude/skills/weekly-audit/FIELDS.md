## Value states and the rules that bind every figure

Every value a figure reads is in exactly one of five states:

- **known**: the field is present with a usable value (a non-negative integer or finite number, or a string of the writers' vocabulary); a known 0 is a **measured zero** and is shown as `0 (measured zero)` in the detail.
- **not applicable**: allowed only by an explicit record value or a documented compatibility rule whose applicability is established from the record. The complete list is in "Not applicable, and why" below; nothing else is ever not applicable.
- **unknown**: the field is absent, or recorded as JSON null, or recorded as the writer's own word `unknown`.
- **invalid**: the field is present with the wrong kind (a string for a number, a boolean, a negative count, a fraction where a count belongs, a value outside the writer's vocabulary, two fields that must agree and do not, a number beyond the reader's range: "The reader's range", below). An invalid present value stays invalid: no rule reads it as another state.
- A total is **complete** only when every input it declares is known or not applicable. Otherwise it reads `unknown; known subtotal X`, in the main report and in the detail alike, and every unknown or invalid input is named in the detail with the parts it affects. Missing never means zero.
- A total's state depends on its inputs' values alone. That another record names a record as evidence changes no value, no state and no coverage label ("Admission" below).

**Scope of a gap.** An input whose content is unknown (a structural gap) counts only against the parts of the figures its kind could feed, and only against the run, helper, class and idle source it could belong to. An unreadable or vanished record, or a line whose event kind cannot be established (not JSON, no event name, a name outside the known list), feeds every part, but only that record's run. An undated line, or a close or opening whose lane is unusable, feeds the parts of its kind (table below) in each interval its time reaches, the run of its record and the helper its own `lane` names (else every helper of that run). A close with no opening before it, timed before its opening, or following an unresolved close or opening of its helper feeds the episode parts in its interval with the same scoping. Each rendered total counts only the gaps that reach it, so a damaged source leaves an independent source's totals known:

- **Class.** An episode's class is its close's, else its opening's, and two that differ conflict. A damaged or unpaired opening or close therefore reaches only the class its own `class` names when no other opening or close of its helper on the record (of any helper there, when its own `lane` is unusable) names a different class or an unusable one; otherwise it reaches every class. The reviewing-class counts and the positional groups count only the gaps that could reach a reviewing class (critic, verifier, gate-judge) or every class: an undated close of class `builder` leaves them complete.
- **Idle source.** A damaged boundary reaches only the idle sum of the source its own `idle_src` names (the unrecorded group when it is absent or null), or every source when its `idle_src` is unusable; a source it names gets its row, unknown, even with no dated boundary of that source in the interval. Every other boundary figure counts it.
- **Spend basis.** A close or beat whose recorded basis is known but whose amount is missing or invalid reaches only the priced total; only one whose basis is unknown or invalid reaches the counts of the other bases (plan usage, unpriced, not applicable, beats not priced) and the plan token totals.

| Event kind | Parts it feeds |
|---|---|
| `lane-open`, `lane-resumed`, `lane-closed` and the historical names read as them | Helper time; Failed closes; Review activity and possible loops (reviewing-class episodes, positional groups); Refusals (helper tool, helper fence, helper sandbox); Priced spend (closes) |
| `phase-boundary` | Phase boundaries (with its rewrites snapshots); Refusals (head fence, boundary summary); Priced spend (snapshots) |
| `gate` | Review activity and possible loops (positional groups); Priced spend (snapshots) |
| `hold-beat` | Priced spend (hold beats) |
| `head-exit`, `run-close` | Priced spend (snapshots) |
| `lane-awake` and `stop-requested` (Episodes), and every other known name | none |

## The reader's range

No record content can stop the report. Every value the reader takes from a record reaches only operations that cannot raise on it, or is named invalid where it stands, and the report completes with every independent figure kept (the class of CR-WASTE4 finding 1, closed by route in B-WASTE13's fold; the routes, and the two shown not to arise, are in that lane's report). So does every value the reader computes from one: a key, a sort key, a group, a label, a formatted figure (B-WASTE15's fold, after CR-WASTE5 finding 1 found a derived route, a recorded class read as a label; each derived route is traced in `evidence-B-WASTE15/routes-traced.md`, and `T4.generated-records-complete-both-reports` holds the class to many generated records at once). CR-WASTE6 found one instance each of three families the traces had missed, an identity read back out of a string built from recorded values, a format the specification names read short of it, and a label sharing a recorded value's place; B-WASTE16's fold closed each family, traced in `evidence-B-WASTE16/routes-traced.md`, and the generated records now hold arbitrary record names and a disposition history too.

- **Numbers.** A recorded number the reader sums, rounds or renders is read only within the reader's range: a magnitude below 10^15 (a thousand million million) and at most 30 places after the decimal point, as written. Any other is invalid, "beyond the reader's range (magnitude too large)" or "(too many decimal places)", named in the detail with the parts it affects, and its figure reads unknown with its known subtotal kept, as for any invalid value. A zero has no magnitude, so only its places can lie beyond. The rule covers every numeric field the reader consumes, the 31 rows (the previous 27 plus the beat's four aggregate token counts) of the matrix below that hold a count, an amount or a time (`T2.field-state-matrix-complete` holds each to a case beyond the range): `duration_s`; `denials.tool`, `denials.fence`, `denials.sandbox`, `sandbox_denials` and `fence_denials_transcript`; a close's and a beat's `total_cost_usd` (on a recorded `result` basis the price is invalid; with no `cost_src`, or a null one, the basis is, as for a negative price); `usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens` and `usage.calls`; the boundary's `idle_min`, `over_cap_reports`, `rewrites` (a delta, its magnitude bounded whatever its sign), `rewrites_total`, `head_denials` and `denials`; and the snapshots `run_spend_usd` (gate, boundary, run-close), `spend_usd` and `spent_usd`; and the evidence used to read a result-basis session (Priced spend): a close's per-model `costUSD` and its four token counts (`inputTokens`, `outputTokens`, `cacheReadInputTokens`, `cacheCreationInputTokens`) in `usage.modelUsage`, and a beat's `usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens`, `usage.cache_read_input_tokens` and `usage.cache_creation_input_tokens`. A value beyond the range leaves that evidence unusable and the session's spend unknown, with a named dependent gap; it is never compared or rendered as a usable amount.
- **Why these bounds, and their headroom.** Set by judgement, unmeasured. Past them a single value aborted the whole report in the build before this one (the main report never written): a sum past 10^999999 overflows the decimal context (a duration of 1e999999999); a total whose rounding needs more than the context's 28 digits cannot be rounded (the minutes of a duration of 1e30, a price of 1e27 to the cent); the exact rendering of a value whose exponent runs to millions of places cannot be built (a duration of 1e-99999999999999); and a sum of counts past 4,300 digits cannot be turned into text (Python 3.11 and later). Within them every sum of recorded decimals is exact: each addition runs under a precision taken from its two operands' own digits, enough for every digit of the exact sum, so none is rounded away whatever the number of addends (under the default context's 28 digits, 0.025 + 1e-30 was 0.025, CR-WASTE5 finding 2); a figure is rounded once, where it is displayed, from the exact total (minutes half-even to 0.1, as the fraction seconds/6 rounded to whole tenths; dollars half-even to the cent, under a precision taken from the total); and a recorded value renders in at most 47 characters. At the records-only snapshot of 19:53:33 BST on 14 October 2026 (251 records, 14,699 lines, 19,432 numeric values of these fields), the largest magnitude was 110,144,929 (one close's `usage.input_tokens`) and the most places 19 (one beat's price, 0.0024432000000000004), none beyond either bound: seven orders of magnitude and 11 places of headroom (`evidence-B-WASTE13/value-survey.txt`).
- **Timestamps.** A fraction of a second holds at most nine digits (nanoseconds, the finest any clock on the writers' machines reports); a longer one is no usable timestamp, named like any other. Set by judgement, unmeasured: the writers write whole seconds (lane.py:2298-2299; handsoff.py:1343-1344) and no line of that snapshot has a fraction. The bound keeps the integer read of the digits from raising (past 4,300 digits, Python 3.11 and later). An accepted fraction is rendered in fixed point to its last digit, .000000001 and .0000001 included (it was dropped in scientific notation before, CR-WASTE5 finding 3).
- **Lines.** A line holding a number the decimal library cannot represent (an exponent beyond about 10^18, such as 1e9999999999999999999, whose error the line parse did not catch) is not JSON, named like a NaN, wherever the number sits. A line holding a string with an unpaired UTF-16 surrogate is not JSON either, whether the string is a value or a member's name, at any depth: JSON's `\u` escapes can write one, but it is no character, and a report holding it cannot be written as UTF-8. An escaped pair is one character, read and shown. The snapshot held neither.
- **The store.** A line of `dispositions.jsonl` nested past the interpreter's depth, or holding such a string, is named "not JSON" like any unreadable line, so the store reads malformed (exit 1, both files written). A run-ended determination is keyed by the field its kind is checked to hold (`run`), and an abandoned one by the opening it names, so another field the line carries is never hashed or ordered.
- **Identities, never strings read back** (CR-WASTE6 finding 1). An episode is its record, helper and opening line, held as that structure. The held format names it by a string, `<record>:<helper>:<line>`, and a record or a helper may hold ":", so one string can name several openings: `r:x:y:1` is both `r.jsonl`'s helper `x:y` and `r:x.jsonl`'s helper `y`. A determination's string is read every way it can be, and applies only where exactly one reading could be an opening of an admitted record (a reading onto a record that cannot be read, or onto a line whose kind cannot be established, could be one); a string naming several openings applies to none of them, and the report names it as ambiguous with each opening it could name. A line the reader's own `abandon` writes also carries the structured identity, `record` (the file name), `helper` and `line`, beside the string, so it names one opening whatever its string could; `end-run` writes the `record` beside the run. Wherever an episode's key could name another opening, the detail shows its record, helper and line beside it. A determination's record extent (its last event) is compared as an instant, never as text: a whole-second local time was the same text for two instants and other text for the same instant in another offset form. An interval's label, which every disposition key carries, names each end to the second with its offset where the minute alone would not tell two intervals apart (a start a store line set within a minute, or a wall time the clock repeats). A record a subcommand names must be a name of the folder's listing exactly, case and Unicode form included. The cases: `T3.colliding-episode-strings-hide-no-opening`, `T3.string-whose-other-record-cannot-be-read-applies-nowhere`, `T3.determination-extent-compared-as-an-instant`, `T8.abandon-names-an-opening-its-string-cannot`, `T8.disposition-keys-name-one-interval`, `T8.subcommands-name-records-exactly`, and the generated records.
- **The held format's abandoned line** (CR-WASTE6 finding 2). The held design's line (its README, "The dispositions store": `episode`, `evidence`, `at`, `last_event`, `record_last_event`) is read as that format writes it: it applies as any other, its evidence listed, and it is checked by what its fields hold (the opening still open on a valid record whose last event is the one recorded and not after the determination). It has no line count of its record, so an event added later at or before that last event (the same second, or out of order) cannot be seen, and its effect says so. A line the reader writes carries `record_lines` too, and is voided by any later line. The cases: `T8.held-format-abandoned-line-applied-its-limit-named` and `T8.held-format-store-lines-read-as-specified` (a store of the held format's lines, one of each kind it names).
- **Shown not to arise.** A value rendered as recorded (a boundary's `from`, by its representation) cannot exhaust the interpreter's depth: the line parse that accepted it ran deeper in the reader's stack than the rendering does (`T4.deepest-nesting-renders`, a guard). Local times built from the earliest and latest stamps the parse accepts (years 1 to 9999, offsets to 23:59 either way) render (`T4.extreme-recorded-years-render`, a guard). Every lookup that hashes a recorded value and every ordering of recorded values runs on a value checked to be a string first, or on a number compared only after its kind is checked.

## Recorded strings and the reader's labels

Whether a value is uncertain is carried in a field of its own, never read back from a string a record could hold (CR-WASTE5 finding 1). A recorded string is a value like any other, whatever its text: a class "(builder)" or "(class invalid)" is a usable class, an `idle_src` "(source unrecorded)" is a source of its own, and no recorded string is ever read as one of the reader's labels.

- **The labels.** Where the reports print one of the reader's own words in place of a recorded value, it is a parenthesised phrase: `(class invalid)`, `(class conflicting)` and `(class unrecorded)` for an unusable class (the episode's class key carries that state), `(source unrecorded)` for the idle source of a boundary whose `idle_src` is absent or null (the source key carries it), `(unusable)` for the helper of an end marker whose `lane` is unusable, and `(all)` for the total row of each helper-time view in the detail, below the rows of the recorded runs, classes and helpers it sums (it was `all`, which a recorded class `all` and a record `all.jsonl` print as: CR-WASTE6 finding 3). In a boundary's or a beat's line in the detail, a field absent or null as recorded shows as `None`. The reader keeps every one of them in one definition, `LABELS`, and the suite takes its list of labels from it (`T2.labels-in-one-definition-the-suite-takes` also holds `LABELS` to the labels' own constants and to a list written out by hand, and finds no string constant of the reader in a label's shape outside it).
- **Recorded strings in those places.** A recorded string there is shown as written unless it could read as such a label or as another value's rendering: one that is one of the labels, begins with a parenthesis or a quotation mark, has white space at either end or holds a character that does not print (a line break, a zero-width space) is shown by its repr, which begins with a quotation mark. So the class "(builder)" shows as `'(builder)'`, the class "(class unrecorded)" as `'(class unrecorded)'`, a record `(all).jsonl` as the run `'(all)'`, a blank `idle_src` as `' '`, and `critic` and `all` as written. The places: the class (the main report's class rows, the detail's class view, the episodes' class cells), the run (the main report's run rows and the detail's run view, and the run that begins a helper's name in both helper views, `'(all)'/P`), the idle source (the main report's idle rows), the helper of a line under a historical event name, and the detail's boundary and beat lines (`idle_src`, `head_denials_src`, `cost_src` and every other field shown as recorded there; `from` and `to` are shown by their repr, as before). A helper's name in a view is its run, `/` and its lane: a run holds no `/` and its shown form adds none, so the first `/` ends the run, and no helper's name is a label.
- **Gap groups.** A named gap's group in the coverage section is carried by its reason, set where the reason is made: the reason with each recorded value it quotes replaced by '…' (a count by N). The reason's text, which quotes recorded values, is never parsed to find it, and the command's summary prints the rows the figures marked for it, never rows found by their text.
- **Elsewhere.** Other recorded strings (a lane, an item, a status outside the writers' vocabulary) are shown in places no label shares, after fixed words (`helper`, `item`, `status outside the writers' vocabulary:`), as written.
- **Line ends.** No recorded value starts a line of either report: a character that ends a line (a line break, a carriage return, a line or paragraph separator) is shown by its escape, `\n` for a line break, so a recorded lane or evidence cannot print a row of its own, a total row (`- (all): …`) or a placeholder (`- none`) among them (`T2.recorded-line-break-starts-no-line`). Before this, a recorded line break broke a detail line's layout (a known limit of B-WASTE15's build).
- **Cases.** `T2.parenthesised-class-completes-the-report` (the review's own lines) and `T2.recorded-strings-equal-to-every-label` (a recorded string equal to every label and display word, `all` and `total` among them, each in the class, the idle source, an end marker's lane and the boundary's recorded fields, beside the real unusable classes and the real unrecorded source: every figure fixed, each value in its own row and line); `T1.view-total-apart-from-recorded-all` (a run, class and helper `all` and `(all)` beside an independent one, every row and the three views' total rows fixed by hand); `T2.labels-in-one-definition-the-suite-takes`; `T2.recorded-line-break-starts-no-line`; `T4.generated-records-complete-both-reports` (arbitrary record names, `all` and `(all)` among them, and every view's total row once, last and apart).

## Inputs: candidates, admission, lines, timestamps

- **Candidates.** Every `*.jsonl` in `<store>/spawn-records/`, the store resolved as `handsoff.py` resolves it: `os.environ.get("LLM_WIKI_STORE") or os.path.join("~", ".llm-wiki")`, real path (handsoff.py:1001-1004; lane.py:563-566 the same). `--records DIR` overrides. Every other entry of the folder (a report, a brief, a prompt, a log, a table, a lane's input folder) is not a candidate; the report counts them and opens none.

### Admission

The owner's input contract, decision B17 of 14 October 2026 (`decisions.md` of run-20261013-build; spec-D19.md, "Input contract (the owner's decision B17, 14 October 2026)", which governs where it and the specification's Inputs paragraph differ). The owner-approved text, as the specification quotes it:

> "For the weekly report, the run-record inputs are every direct `.jsonl` file in the records folder except the five historical event-log names explicitly listed in the input contract. The reader acquires those records independently and never follows references to obtain further inputs. An admitted record remains an input when another record names it as evidence; that reference alone is neither a prohibited read nor incomplete figure coverage. Excluded evidence files remain unopened, including through subprocesses. Producers must keep evidence outside the admitted namespace. This contract relies on that separation and does not promise to detect misfiled evidence before opening it."

It replaced head 2's provisional R1, whose breach flag and partial-coverage marking went with it (B17's own reading); R1's text stays in `stopped-design-options.md`. Admission is a classification by name, decided for every candidate, from its name alone, before any file is opened.

A hidden `*.jsonl` name is a candidate like any other (the folder is listed, not globbed).
- **Not admitted:** the five historical GPT-6 Astra event logs pinned by exact name (the comparison is exact, case included; all five are in the store):
  - `run-20260909-n14-accept-events-L1.jsonl`
  - `run-20260909-n14-accept-events-L2.jsonl`
  - `run-20260909-n14-accept-events-L3.jsonl`
  - `run-20260909-n14-accept-events-L5.jsonl`
  - `run-20260909-n14-accept-events-L6.jsonl`

  Each is never opened, and is listed by name in the coverage section of every report and in the detail's inventory, its contents unread and unknown. It is no run record, so no figure reads it and it is no named gap. A folder holding no admitted name is a failed premise (exit 2).
- **Not admitted, by the owner's extension of B17 (approved by the owner on 25 October 2026 at 20:32:24 GMT, "yes for 1", to the question whether his admission rule of 14 October may be extended to the command checker's read logs):** the command checker's read logs, each named `<run>-unplaced-reads.jsonl`, `<run>` a run id of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,128}`, beside its run's record `<run>.jsonl` (lane-fence.py: `UNPLACED_LOG_SUFFIX`, `RUN_ID`, and `record_unplaced_reads`, which appends one line per cleared command holding a read it cannot place, beside the run's record and only while `<run>.jsonl` is a regular file there). Its lines hold `v`, `ts`, `who`, `sid`, `cwd`, `n`, `reads`, `cmd` and `cut` and no `event`: read as a run record, each was a line with no event name, a gap of every figure of its run (274 such lines reached the week of 18 to 25 October 2026; on 25 October 2026 the folder held 7 such logs, 3,044 lines, every one with exactly those nine members). The logger is a producer keeping evidence inside the admitted namespace, the case the contract names ("Producers must keep evidence outside the admitted namespace"). The rule reads the names alone, before any file is opened: a name of that form is left out only when `<run>.jsonl` is in the same listing; with no such record beside it, it is a run record like any other (CR-WW1 finding 6: a run whose own id ends in `-unplaced-reads` has a read log's name, and the logger writes one only beside its run's record, so a real run record is never left out unseen). Each file left out is left out as the pinned event logs are: never opened, listed by name in the coverage section of every report (the line says how many of each kind, and lists every name left out, so a record left out beside a record of the run it would log is seen) and in the detail's inventory, its contents unread and unknown, no run record and no named gap; a subcommand refuses it as a run. A name that ends so but whose prefix is no run id (one holding a space, say) is admitted. The owner's text above lists the five event logs; the extension adds the read logs beside them and changes nothing else in the contract. On 25 October 2026 at 21:24 UTC the folder held 8 such logs, 3,306 lines, each beside its run's record, and no name of that form without one.
- **Never opened at all:** any other extension, any pinned event log, any path outside the records folder (a file in a subfolder of it included, being no direct entry), and so any file a record references outside the inputs. A candidate whose directory entry is a symbolic link is never followed (its target could lie outside the folder or be a pinned event log), and one that is not a regular file is never opened: each is examined by `lstat`, which opens nothing, and named **unreadable**, a gap of every figure of its own run in every report; the open itself refuses a link (`O_NOFOLLOW`) and cannot block on a pipe (`O_NONBLOCK`).
- **A record another record names as evidence: read as a record like any other.** The reader acquires the admitted records independently and never reads a reference: no field of any line is read as a path, resolved, looked up or followed. A record that another record names as evidence, in any field and any spelling (`event_log`, `events`, `transcript`, `report`, a nested `paths.report`, a list, a relative path, a `~` path), is read once, as a run record, by the rules of this file and nothing else (B17: "that reference alone is neither a prohibited read nor incomplete figure coverage"). What a reference may and may not change (spec-D19.md, "Input contract", the bullet of that name): a reference, and an excluded file named by one, change no figure, no coverage status and no other substantive report content; the acquisition time, the measured byte counts and the folder inventory (each file's own listing line) are reported as they are, so adding or removing a reference field, or removing an excluded file inside the folder, changes those lines and nothing else. A file outside the inputs that a record names is never opened, whatever it holds, so changing it changes no figure, no coverage status and no other substantive report content; and a reference inside a file that is never read (a pinned event log, another extension) is never seen. The cases check more than that promise: on a fixed clock, references replaced by dashes of their own length leave both reports byte for byte the same, and removing an excluded file in the folder changes exactly its own listing lines (`T5.admitted-record-named-as-evidence-changes-nothing`, `T5.excluded-file-named-as-evidence-never-opened`; `T5.reference-promise-as-the-contract-states` holds this file and README.md to the promise's words). An evidence file misfiled under an admitted name is read as a run record, its lines most likely invalid and named as gaps of its own run, never parsed as evidence: the contract "does not promise to detect misfiled evidence before opening it" (`T5.event-stream-under-an-admitted-name-read-as-a-record`).
- **Why the name decides.** A run's record is `<run>.jsonl` (lane.py:577; handsoff.py:1339-1340, `record_path`), and every other file a writer puts in the folder has another extension: `-definition-`, `-appended-`, `-report-` (`.md`), `-settings-` (`.json`), `-in-` (a folder), `-detached-`, `-events-`, `-stderr-` (`.log`), `-prompt-` (`.md`) (lane.py:573-583; codex-lane.py:976-984, whose comment at 979-980 reads "`.log`, not `.jsonl`: the readers that glob the records directory for `*.jsonl` (lane.py's cost fold, deep-lint's spawn-record review) must never take an event stream for a record"). The writers accept any plain token `[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,128}` as a run id (handsoff.py:964, 1333-1340), so no rule on the run id can tell a record from evidence; only the extension can, and the five historical event logs that carry a `.jsonl` name are the known exceptions, pinned, with the command checker's read logs, the one writer that puts evidence under the extension (the rule above). The producers' side of the contract, "Producers must keep evidence outside the admitted namespace", is a rule for the record writers: a register entry, since this run changes no writer (spec-D19.md, "Input contract").
- **Lines.** Each line is one JSON object (lane.py:2251-2259 and handsoff.py:1455-1463 append `json.dumps(payload) + "\n"`). A line must parse strictly (no duplicated member, no NaN or Infinity, no number the decimal library cannot represent, no string holding an unpaired surrogate: "The reader's range"), carry a string `event` in the known list, and a usable `ts`. Blank lines are skipped. Every failure is kept and named, never repaired. The known list holds every name the granted writers emit and every name present in the store at this build (88 names in the store, plus `unwatched`, lane.py:3608), `lane-awake`, which both wrappers write and which joined it on 25 October 2026 (Episodes), and `stop-requested`, which lane.py writes and which joined it the same day (Episodes); the script holds it as `KNOWN_EVENTS`.
- **Timestamps.** The writers write `time.strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S%z")` (lane.py:2298-2299; handsoff.py:1343-1344; codex-lane.py:259-260 delegates to lane.py). Accepted: `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM[:SS[.fraction]]` with `Z`, `+hhmm` or `+hh:mm`, every field in range, a fraction of at most nine digits ("The reader's range"), exact (fractions kept, and rendered to their last digit in fixed point; nothing normalised). A stamp with no offset is not accepted: every writer writes one, and a local reading would depend on the reader's zone. Store shapes: `…T99:99:99+9999` 13,923 lines, `…T99:99+9999` 599, `…T99:99+99:99` 9, `…T99:99:99+99:99` 8.
- **Undated lines.** Placed only inside a record whose dated lines are in non-decreasing order (every writer appends in time order), between its dated neighbours; in an unordered record they reach every interval. Either way the record is named in the detail of every report until mended.

## Episodes

- **Opening:** `lane-open` (lane.py:4095-4097; codex-lane.py:1087-1091, 1144) or `lane-resumed` (lane.py:4755, written before its process at lane.py:3230-3238; codex-lane.py:1408). **Close:** `lane-closed` (lane.py:3312-3313, 3244-3252, 3477-3478; codex-lane.py:915-919, 941-942, 1632-1636). Both keyed by the record and the line's `lane`, a non-blank string. Store: `lane-open` 2,251, `lane-resumed` 461, `lane-closed` 2,692, each with a string `lane`.
- **Pairing.** A valid close pairs with the latest valid opening of its lane before it on the record: "the close that counts is the one after the latest open or resume" (lane.py:159-160). At most one close per episode, and only valid lines pair:
  - an opening replaced by a later opening without a valid close stays named, open;
  - a close with no opening before it, or timed before its opening, is named and counted in no figure (the opening stays open);
  - a close whose own line is invalid (no usable timestamp) resolves nothing: a malformed close is not evidence that an opening resolved (the stopped design's item 5(b)). The opening is named **unresolved**; a later valid close of the same helper pairs with nothing and is named; a later opening starts a new episode;
  - an opening whose own line is invalid opens no episode, and a close after it pairs with nothing and is named.
- **Counting.** An episode counts wholly in the interval holding its close's `ts`; an open or unresolved episode counts nowhere and is named in every report, however old, opened after the interval included.
- **Resumed.** Each pass writes its own opening, its own process and its own close (`run_lane` is shared by spawn and resume: lane.py:3221; codex-lane.py:907), and lane.py marks the resumed close `resumed: true, resume_n` (lane.py:4773). A resumed helper is therefore several distinct episodes, each with its own duration; nothing is added twice. A Claude pass's recorded `total_cost_usd` is, from 19 September 2026, the running total of its session, earlier passes included, and before then the pass's own cost; each session is read from its own lines (Priced spend), so the priced figure counts what each session spent, never one pass twice. `lane-spawned` and `lane-respawned` mark a process start after its opening (lane.py:2291, 4752; codex-lane.py:1426) and open nothing. `lane-awake` follows either when the wrapper starts its keep-awake process beside the lane process (lane.py's `start_awake` and the line written after `lane-spawned`, lane.py:5227-5256 and 5833-5840; codex-lane.py:1394-1401; both as read on 25 October 2026): `awake` is `started`, with `caffeinate_pid`, or `skipped`, with the why in `note`; `watched_pid` is the lane's process and `command` the keep-awake command, beside `ts`, `run`, `lane`, `session_id`, `wrapper_pid`, `class` and `detached` (codex-lane.py adds `harness`). It is a known event that feeds no figure: it opens, closes and resolves nothing, and no field of it is read. A held-lane beat writes none, its keep-awake state riding on its `hold-beat` line (lane.py:5259-5268). `stop-requested` (lane.py's `a3_request_stop`, lane.py:4345-4346 as read on 25 October 2026) records that a stop was asked of a running pass: `run`, `lane`, `pass_id` and `intent` (its `cause`, `reason`, `explicit`, `ts` and `requester`), beside `ts`. It is a known event that feeds no figure: it opens, closes and resolves nothing, and no field of it is read; the pass's close, or its absence, is what the figures read. `lane-swapped` marks a closed lane's work moving to a new lane that has its own opening and close (lane.py:2898) and resolves nothing.
- **Interrupted.** A close with exit `limit`, `deadline`, `stall`, `killed`, `watch-closed`, `failed`, `error`, `budget`, `refusal` or `empty` is an ordinary close of a non-completed episode; its recorded duration and cost stand, and no figure says its work was lost.

## Known gaps in the record fields (findings, not patched)

- Older records can report a resumed pass's own cost or its session's running total, and no line names the harness's version. The reader requires usable session evidence rather than assuming a cut-over date (Priced spend). A single model whose every measure rises can fit either reading and remains unknown. Transcript token counts (`usage.tokens`) do not settle that ambiguity: lane.py has counted them both over the whole transcript and over the current pass alone (`a3_suffix`), without a field naming the convention. Missing or contradictory history remains unknown even when a later line has a usable total. A writer field declaring the accounting basis, supported by complete records, would resolve the remaining ambiguity; the earlier historical classification survey is not evidence for the current classifier.
- Inventory completeness is checked from the recorded structure and, on a priced close, by reconciling its model costs with its total (Priced spend, the head's decision of 27 October 2026): every named model must have all required fields, every beat its full model list and aggregate counts, and a priced close's costs must sum to its `total_cost_usd` within 1E-9 USD. A wholly omitted model entry is caught when its cost exceeds that tolerance, as one token at the cheapest list rate does (1E-7 USD); an omitted entry costing at most 1E-9 USD, a zero-cost one say, changes no amount and passes. The tolerance rests on the rounding measured in the records as they stood on 27 October 2026 (at most 6.4E-14 USD): a producer that rounded its totals more coarsely, or a list rate a hundredfold below Haiku's cache-read rate, would need the rule revisited. A beat's inventory carries no costs and is checked by structure alone. The accounting decision uses the recorded model identities, usable measures and session history, and cannot certify evidence the producer did not record.
- lane.py's ordinary close has no sandbox channel (lane.py:3327-3328): the helper sandbox total is unknown in any week with a Claude close.
- codex-lane.py's kill close records no `duration_s` and no `denials` (codex-lane.py:1632-1636).
- codex-lane.py writes `denials.tool` and `denials.fence` as constant zeros (codex-lane.py:959) rather than marking them not applicable.
- No opening or close names its item or a review round: the positional group is a cue, and rounds stay unresolved.
- A head-exit's `spent_usd` is null on 56 of 122 head-exits, and no head-exit in the store carries `spent_src`, so a null cannot be told from an unmeasured meter by the record alone (handsoff.py:6137-6138 writes `spent_src` on the limit stop only).
- Closes written before `cost_src` existed carry a price on an undeclared basis; hand-written closes often lack `exit_class`, `duration_s` and `class`; hand-written episode lines use the historical names above.
- No writer rule keeps evidence out of the `.jsonl` namespace of the records folder: codex-lane.py moved its event stream to `.log` (codex-lane.py:976-984), but five historical event logs keep a `.jsonl` name (pinned here), the command checker's read logging writes `<run>-unplaced-reads.jsonl` there (left out by name beside its run's record since 25 October 2026, the owner's extension of B17), and nothing stops a writer or a hand from writing another. An evidence file under an admitted name is read as a run record (its lines most likely invalid and named). The owner's contract relies on the producers' side ("Producers must keep evidence outside the admitted namespace") and "does not promise to detect misfiled evidence before opening it"; that rule is a register entry for the writers, since this run changes no writer.

## Unexpected-failure containment

These scopes apply to unexpected exceptions only; ordinary missing-field and invalid-value selectivity above is unchanged. The failure boundary preserves dedicated control/validation exceptions (`Probe`, `Bad`, `NotOpened`) and contains other `Exception` subclasses. A failure names cached physical sources, route and exception class in both reports. No exception message is rendered. Source identities include blank physical positions and are allocated before blank/decode processing. Validated kind/time facts are committed separately; payloads and envelopes are immutable across replay.

| Trusted source or failed operation | Withdrawn contributions, across that record | Independent contributions retained |
|---|---|---|
| Any `ALL_OPENINGS` / `ALL_CLOSES` member; pairing, episode class/value, episode/detail row | Helper time; close/status/resumed counts; reviewing-class and positional groups; helper refusal channels; close spend, the record's priced spend by session (its beats with it: a beat is a line of its session) and every plan/basis/token count; episode membership/open state | Other records, and this record's boundary families and beat basis counts |
| `phase-boundary` | Boundary counts, idle-source views, rewrite deltas/snapshots, head/summary refusals, spend snapshots | Episode consumption, helper refusals and beat charges |
| `gate` | Positional activity and spend snapshots, including later episodes | Duration, reviewing-class count and episode charges |
| `hold-beat` | Beat spend and beat basis counts, and the record's priced spend by session (its closes with it: a close is a line of its session) | Episode-only charges other than priced spend, and other families |
| Priced spend by session (`spend-session`, one collection per record, its anchor the owner, its closes and beats before the interval's end and the lines that could be lost to its sessions the suppliers; a record whose priced closes or beats any contained failure withdrew joins none) | That record's priced closes and beats in the priced total | Every other figure, and other records' sessions |
| `head-exit` | Spend snapshots | Episode and boundary values |
| `run-open`, `run-resume`, `run-close` | Run state/coverage; also spend snapshots for `run-close` | Independently established episode consumption |
| `lane-spawned`, `lane-respawned`, `lane-swapped` | Event-derived coverage/eligibility; no episode-opening or resumed-status dependency | Episode figures |
| Historical end markers | Historical notes/counts and related open-state explanation/eligibility; never a new close | Measured closed consumption |
| Other trusted known names | Event-derived coverage and eligibility | Numeric families unless a combined operation declares them |
| Kind unknown; unexpected timestamp/metadata failure | Every figure family and event-derived coverage domain | Other records and known acquisition facts |
| Combined collection, row, key or formatter | Declared conservative domain over all participating sources; never a guessed single culprit | Disjoint domains; a global aggregate can name several records |

All contained record failures also make that record's determination eligibility uncertain. No abandonment or run-ending determination applies to a damaged record. Uncertainty witnesses are deduplicated by physical source/domain, not interpreted as a count of lost episodes. A source outside the interval can change an earlier close's resumed status, so containment is never filtered by that source's timestamp. Helper-view rankings and affected coverage cannot claim a definite zero, absence, completeness or outside-interval status after withdrawal.

| Failed trusted store kind | Uncertain authority | Output/persistence consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `init` | Initialisation/continuity | Malformed continuity, exit 1 |
| `report` | Default start and since-previous chronology | Without explicit start: diagnostic reports, no interval evaluation or append; with explicit start: normal interval and unknown chronology |
| `listed` | Carried-key inventory | Retain observed keys; membership/inventory incomplete, no invented lost key |
| `disposition` | Current/rerun and carried figure dispositions | Every relevant key undetermined; older hidden observations reappear |
| `abandoned` | Episode exclusion | No exclusion; candidate openings and adjusted counts unknown |
| `run-ended` | Run endings | No application; candidate endings and adjusted coverage unknown |
| Untrusted kind | Union of all authorities | No authority inferred from an uncommitted field |
| Outgoing serialisation/encoding | Persistence | Entire append suppressed, both reports name the failure, exit 1 |

The route catalogue is `ROUTES` in the reader. Every ordinary line operation and attributed collection is guarded; the complete generation is the publication candidate. A failed slot is terminal and its generation is discarded. Only immutable input envelopes, acquisition facts, cached safe labels and monotone failure state survive. Output preparation includes both reports, stdout and encoded store bytes; no data-dependent formatting follows the freeze. The trusted terminal emitter and allocation/I/O limits are described in README.md.

The `store-entry` route owns one physical store line and covers construction and tentative publication. Failure rolls back both the entry and its cached listed-key observation; the trusted kind still determines authority uncertainty. If combined key preparation fails, the terminal history retains all committed listed identities, including previously disposed and undecidable carry candidates, alongside current identities, with membership and status unknown. It does not report an empty since-previous history.

Recovery lists observed identities separately from membership and disposition decisions. Both reports retain committed listed identities and every final current identity whenever history is appended. Successfully prepared statuses and historical entries survive unrelated failures, including whole-report and encoding fallbacks; only failure of the combined `keys` preparation makes history preparation unavailable. An empty prepared history remains unknown when chronology or disposition authority is uncertain, and is a known zero otherwise. Individual store-authority flags still govern the prepared decisions, and a failed history-row presentation marks that row unavailable without hiding other entries. A separately validated and published listed entry contributes its key even if earlier kind extraction failed; the authority uncertainty remains. The terminal emitter uses primitive identities, statuses and validated history strings with its own escaping; it never depends on the failed ordinary formatter. Unresolved-start diagnostics and unavailable generation/outgoing preparation append no history.

Run candidates carry an explicit `open` or `unknown` state; affected record eligibility or run-ending authority makes the latter propagate to main coverage and detail. A positional group's owner is its first episode's opening source, while its supplier set remains every contributing episode source. A failed group therefore cannot terminalise an independent group's presentation. Per-run table coverage counts rendered links from the final detail candidate; a failed terminal presentation has zero rendered links and names the unavailable presentation.

Invalid-line row and inventory availability are separate from an empty section: terminal presentation emits unknown with its cached locator. Table-selection availability is separate from the rendered-link count: a failed selector emits unknown selection and zero rendered links, while an available empty selection reports none found.

The suffix limit is derived from `sys.maxsize`, the maximum addressable acquired bytes position, rather than Python's configurable conversion threshold. Both rational interval endpoints round-trip through `iso_exact`; labels omit seconds only for an exact whole minute with an unambiguous local reading. Helpers use one ASCII-escaped JSON component representation, before layout, while stored identities and keys retain their existing formats.
